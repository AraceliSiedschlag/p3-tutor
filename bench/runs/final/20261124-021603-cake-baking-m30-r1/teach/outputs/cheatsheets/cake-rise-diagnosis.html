Why Didn't My Cake Rise?

From Lesson 1: What Makes a Cake Rise (objective O2). Keep this one open while you bake.

Three things make a cake rise

  1. Chemical gas — CO2 from baking soda or baking powder reacting with an acid.
  2. Steam and beaten-in air — water turning to steam, plus air you mixed in.
  3. Protein structure — egg and gluten proteins that set (coagulate) and lock the shape in.

All three are needed. Great leavening with no set structure still collapses.

Baking soda vs. baking powder

Baking sodaBaking powder
What it isSodium bicarbonate (a base)Soda + its own acid + starch
Needs an acid from the recipe?Yes (buttermilk, yogurt, vinegar, brown sugar, cocoa...)No — its own acid is built in
ReactsOnce, as soon as it's wetTwice: on mixing, then again on oven heat ("double-acting")
Strength~3x stronger than powder, teaspoon for teaspoonWeaker; not a 1-for-1 swap for soda
Not interchangeable 1-for-1. Swapping one for the other without adjusting the amount and the recipe's acid under-leavens or leaves a bitter/soapy taste.

Diagnosing a sunk or flat cake: the four-question checklist

  1. Enough acid for the soda? No acidic ingredient (buttermilk, yogurt, vinegar, brown sugar, natural cocoa...) → soda stays unreacted → dense, under-risen, soapy taste.
  2. Amount of leavening reasonable? More than the recipe calls for, or a soda/powder swap without adjusting it → gas escapes faster than structure traps it → fast rise, then collapse, bitter/soapy taste.
  3. Oven actually at the right temperature? Check with a thermometer, not the dial — ovens are often off by a lot. Too cool hides underbaking behind a done-looking crust; too hot sets the edges before the middle finishes rising.
  4. Structure given a full, undisturbed bake? Pulled early, or oven door opened mid-bake → structure never finishes setting → looks fine, then sinks as it cools.

Matching symptom to cause

SymptomLikely cause
Domed fast, then fell during baking; bitter/soapy tasteToo much leavener
