Baking and frosting a simple cake

Bake-day order, and why each step sits there

  1. Spoon flour into the cup, then level it. Fixes the amount the recipe meant; scooping packs in extra flour and dries out the cake.
  2. Cream butter and sugar to pale and fluffy, no longer. Cuts the right amount of air into the fat; under- or over-creaming both leave a denser cake.
  3. Bake, then cool completely. A warm cake is too soft to slice or frost cleanly.
  4. Level each cooled layer. Removes the dome so the next layer has a flat, stable base.
  5. Apply a thin crumb coat over the whole cake. Traps loose crumbs before they can travel into the final coat.
  6. Chill the crumb coat until firm, about 30 minutes. A firm coat lets the final layer glide on; a soft one drags and pulls crumbs back out.
  7. Spread the final, decorative frosting coat. Goes last because every step before it built a stable, crumb-free, firm surface.

What goes wrong, and why

Skip or rush thisWhat happens
Scooping flour instead of spooningPacks in extra flour by weight; cake bakes dense and dry
Creaming far past pale and fluffyBeats in too much air, mixture separates; cake bakes dense or uneven
LevelingDome leaves no flat base; layers rock, slide, or crack when stacked
Chilling the crumb coatSoft coat drags under the knife; final coat pulls crumbs back out
The crumb coat's whole job is trapping crumbs against the cake, not decoration; the final coat is what people see.
